Thailand Coatings Market: Opportunities for Waterborne Systems

Published: 2026-11-27 · Censilcoat Technical Team · Market & regulation

The Thailand coatings market is one of the most diversified in Southeast Asia. A single country supports a large automotive manufacturing base, an export-oriented electrical and electronics industry, furniture and wood-product makers, and a steady construction and decorative paint sector. For suppliers of waterborne systems, that mix is the opportunity: each segment is at a different stage of moving away from solvent-borne coatings, and each one asks something slightly different of the raw materials. This article looks at where waterborne technology has the clearest openings in Thailand, what drives the shift, the technical hurdles of formulating in a hot and humid climate, and how to choose and qualify a matting agent for waterborne wood and plastic coatings sold into Thai industry.

It is written for R&D managers at Thai coating plants and for the purchasing teams who source their additives, as well as for regional formulators supplying Thai customers from Vietnam, Malaysia or further afield.

A snapshot of the Thailand coatings market

Rather than repeat market-size estimates from paid reports, which vary widely depending on how each publisher defines the segments, it is more useful to look at the industries that consume coatings. Three stand out.

  • Automotive. Thailand is one of the largest vehicle producers in Southeast Asia and a major exporter. According to the Federation of Thai Industries (FTI), as reported by Khaosod English, vehicle production in the first eleven months of 2025 was 1,341,714 units, and the FTI set a 2026 production target of 1.45 million vehicles, including 950,000 for export. Every vehicle carries OEM paint, plus dozens of coated plastic interior and exterior parts, which supports a deep tier of parts suppliers and plastic coating makers.
  • Electrical, electronics and appliances. Housings, trims and components for appliances and consumer electronics are coated on plastic substrates such as ABS and PC/ABS, where low-gloss, scratch-resistant and soft-touch finishes are common specifications.
  • Furniture, wood products and construction. Wood furniture, doors, flooring and joinery use clear and pigmented wood coatings, while the building sector consumes architectural paints, many of which are already water-based emulsions.

Decorative wall paints moved to water long ago in most markets, including Thailand. The bigger remaining opportunity for waterborne technology lies in industrial segments, wood finishes and plastic coatings, where solvent-borne systems are still widespread and where the move to water is technically harder.

What is driving the shift to waterborne in Thailand

Export customer requirements

A large share of Thai manufacturing is exported, and brand owners in Europe, North America and Japan increasingly specify lower-VOC finishes and ask for restricted-substance declarations. When the final customer changes its specification, the coating supplier is asked to follow, often on a fixed timeline.

Eco-labelling and domestic expectations

Thailand has a national eco-label run by the Thailand Environment Institute. Its Green Label criteria for paints (TGL-4-R4-14) cover both water-based and solvent-based paints and varnishes, set VOC limits in grams per litre that differ by paint type, and restrict heavy metals, formaldehyde and certain solvents. It is a certification scheme that manufacturers apply for, not a general legal limit, but it shows the direction of buyer and procurement expectations. Always check the current version and the Thai original text before claiming compliance.

Workplace and plant considerations

Lower solvent content can reduce flammability and odour in spray booths and storage areas, which matters for plants in dense industrial estates. These benefits depend on the specific formulation and site, so treat them as considerations to evaluate rather than guaranteed outcomes.

Technology maturity

Acrylic emulsions, polyurethane dispersions and hybrid binders have narrowed the performance gap with solvent-borne systems for many wood and plastic applications. The remaining challenges are mostly about application and appearance, and that is where additive choice matters.

Technical hurdles of waterborne matte coatings in a tropical climate

Thailand's heat and high humidity affect waterborne coatings more than solvent-borne ones, because water evaporation and film coalescence depend directly on air conditions. Formulators converting matte systems typically meet these issues:

  1. Whitening and haze at high humidity. Slow water release and incomplete coalescence can leave a milky cast in matte clear coats, and the matting silica adds to the light scatter. Our article on whitening in waterborne matte coatings at high humidity explains how to diagnose this on a dark panel.
  2. Gloss sensitive to drying conditions. Matte films build their surface texture as the film shrinks during drying. Changes in temperature, airflow or film thickness between shifts can shift 60° gloss by several units.
  3. Settling in low-viscosity bases. Waterborne topcoats are often thin. Untreated silica can settle into hard cake during warehouse storage in warm conditions; heat-aged storage tests are essential.
  4. Thin films on plastics. Plastic topcoats are often applied at around 10–15 μm dry. Coarse particles or over-dosing can produce specks, a rough feel and viscosity spikes.
  5. Clarity over dark wood. High matting agent loadings needed for low gloss can cause a grey or hazy look over dark stains.

None of these is unique to Thailand, but the climate makes them appear sooner and more often, so they belong in the test plan from the first lab batch.

Choosing a matting agent for Thai waterborne coatings

Silica matting agents work by creating fine surface roughness as the film dries and shrinks around the particles, scattering reflected light. In waterborne systems, the following properties do most of the work:

  • Particle size (d50 and top size). Coarser grades matt more efficiently; finer grades give better clarity and a smoother touch. For thin plastic films, the particle size must be suited to the dry film thickness.
  • Pore volume. Higher pore volume generally means more particles per gram and higher matting efficiency, balanced against viscosity increase.
  • Surface treatment. Wax-treated silica helps keep sediment soft and stir-back easy and can improve scratch and burnish resistance, both important for warm-climate storage and for automotive or floor applications.
  • Dispersibility. Grades that reach fineness with a high-speed disperser avoid bead milling, which suits plants making many small batches.
  • Documentation. Confirm the grade is amorphous silica on the SDS, and request TDS and COA so you can answer export customer questionnaires.

A practical qualification sequence: record the incumbent's 60° and 85° gloss, clarity, viscosity and Hegman reading in your own base; run the candidate at the same dosage and one or two steps either side with identical dispersion and film build; heat-age sealed samples (commonly 7 days at 50 °C) and check sediment; spray panels under humid conditions; compare two lots; then run a line trial while keeping the incumbent as a dual source. Frequently Asked Questions

1. Which segments of the Thailand coatings market offer the best opportunities for waterborne systems?

Industrial segments where solvent-borne coatings are still common: automotive interior plastic parts, electronics and appliance housings, wood furniture and doors, flooring and general industrial coatings. Decorative wall paints are already largely water-based.

2. Does Thailand have VOC limits for paints?

The Thai Green Label criteria for paints (TGL-4-R4-14), run by the Thailand Environment Institute, set VOC limits in grams per litre by paint type for both water-based and solvent-based products. It is a certification scheme manufacturers apply for rather than a general legal limit, so check current rules for your product with the relevant Thai authorities.

3. Why is humidity a problem for waterborne matte coatings in Thailand?

High humidity slows water evaporation and coalescence. In matte clear coats this can cause whitening or haze, and gloss can drift with drying conditions. Test panels under the humidity your line actually sees and choose humidity-tolerant additives.

4. What matting agent properties matter for thin waterborne plastic coatings?

A particle size suited to the dry film thickness, controlled top size to avoid specks, pore volume for efficient matting without viscosity spikes, wax treatment for soft sediment and scratch resistance, and easy high-speed dispersion.
